Design notes

A messaging-style assistant for StayEasy holiday-let guests. Taps do most of the work; typing always works too; a person is never more than one tap away.

Evidence behind the design

    Design decisions

    • Routine questions to the bot, people for what matters. Letting the assistant handle everyday queries like Wi-Fi, check-in and extras reduces the workload on the human team. That gives them enough time for guests who need a real person, like a refund or cancellation. Those conversations, handled badly, cause churn and damage the brand.
    • Buttons first, typing second. Every step offers quick replies, and typed messages are routed by keyword so guests can skip ahead.
    • Show the policy before the action. Fees and refund amounts are calculated from the stay date and shown before a guest confirms.
    • Two misunderstandings, then a person. After two messages in a row the bot can't place, it offers a person instead of looping.
    • Urgent skips the menus. Words like “leak” or “locked out” jump straight to the amber card, 999 advice and the on-call host.
    • Handover carries context. The agent gets a summary, the booking and the transcript, so the guest never repeats themselves. Toggle Agent view to see it.
    • Wi-Fi by floor. Rose Cottage has a router on each of its three floors, so the bot asks which floor you're on.
